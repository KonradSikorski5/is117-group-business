# Research findings

**Question:** Who would buy a small interior car-care kit with a scent, and what do they need to see before they buy it online?

All three references were checked on their official sites on **2026-11-15**. Links are on [Sources](/sources).

## Evidence vs. assumptions

**Evidence (seen on the pages above):**
1. A direct competitor already sells an interior kit *with* a scent, for $32.30 on sale ($44.96 regular).
2. Free shipping at Chemical Guys starts at $75, which is more than the kit costs, so buying just one kit doesn't qualify.
3. Armor All tells buyers to keep its wipes **in the car**, so "clean up on the go" is something brands already sell.
4. Little Trees says a Vent Wrap lasts **up to 20 days**, so a scent runs out and has to be bought again.
5. None of the three pages mentions students or commuters.

**Assumptions (our guesses, not tested):**
- College commuters in NJ spend a lot of time in their cars and would want a quick, cheap way to keep the inside clean.
- A kit under about $25, with the shipping cost shown up front, would feel easier to buy than a $32–$45 kit with a $75 free-shipping minimum.
- People will want scent refills, because the scent runs out (based on evidence 4, but not tested with buyers).

## Two positions

| Position | What we'd emphasize | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Quick clean for commuters** (chosen) | Small glovebox kit, low price, scent included, full price shown before checkout | Fills a gap: none of the three references talks to commuters or students | Might look too basic next to big-brand kits |
| **B. New-driver gift kit** | Nicer box, gift message, bigger kit | Gifts could support a higher price | Gift buying is seasonal, and it's further from our own experience |

## Two persona hypotheses (not real people)

> These are **hypotheses**, not interviews. They help us decide what the page should show.

- **Primary: commuting college student.** Drives to campus and work, eats in the car, wants the car to look and smell okay when friends get in. Needs a cheap kit and the full price before checkout. Worries about paying shipping on a small order.
- **Secondary (contrast): parent buying for a new driver.** Wants a useful gift. Needs to see what's in the box. Worries about the quality of a no-name brand.

## Chosen audience

**Commuting college students (Position A).** We chose them because of what we saw: none of the three references speaks to students or commuters (evidence 5), and the closest kit costs $32–$45 with free shipping only at $75 (evidence 1–2). That's a gap for a smaller kit with an up-front total. Whether students actually want this is still an assumption.

## From finding to decision to check

| Finding | Decision (proposed feature) | Check (planned, not done yet) |
